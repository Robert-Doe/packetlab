Module 21 — Deep Theory: Research Methodology, Disclosure Ethics, and the CTF Mindset

1. Command-by-Command

python solve_crackme.py ctf_challenge/crackme --verify

What it does: reads the compiled binary's raw bytes, brute-forces all 255 non-zero single-byte XOR keys against a flag{...}-shaped regular expression, and — critically — does not stop there. The --verify step invokes the ACTUAL challenge binary with the recovered candidate flag and checks its real exit code. This closes the gap between "I computed something that looks right" and "I confirmed it is right" — a distinction with real consequences: a regex could coincidentally match a wrong string in a large enough search space, and only the challenge's own logic is authoritative about what the real answer is.

python research_notes_checker.py your_log.json

What it does: the same completeness-and-consistency validation pattern as Module 19's capstone_checker.py, applied to a research writeup's STRUCTURE — does it have a real question, cited related work with stated relevance, a reproducible methodology, an ethics statement, evidence-backed results, and stated limitations. It cannot judge whether your research is GOOD; it can only confirm the standard structural pieces are present.

python disclosure_timeline_checker.py your_timeline.json

What it does: performs REAL date arithmetic (parsing ISO dates, computing day differences) to check a set of disclosure dates against ordering rules (vendor contact must precede public disclosure) and the 90-day standard-window CONVENTION — flagging deviations as needing explicit justification, not automatically condemning them.

2. What "Falsifiable" Actually Means, Precisely

A research question is FALSIFIABLE if there exists some possible observation or result that would show the question's implied hypothesis is WRONG. "Network security monitoring" is not falsifiable — there's no observation that could contradict a topic name. "Does a fixed jitter-ratio threshold produce a false-positive rate above 10% against consumer IoT traffic?" IS falsifiable — a real measured false-positive rate below 10% would directly contradict it. This is the exact property research_notes_checker.py's question-length and question-mark checks are crude proxies for — a real comprehensive exam committee applies the same falsifiability standard far more rigorously than any automated string check could.

Why does falsifiability matter so much in security research specifically? Unfalsifiable claims ("this system is secure") are common in security marketing precisely because they can never be definitively disproven by any single test — a system that hasn't been broken YET is not the same as a system that CAN'T be broken. Falsifiable claims ("this system resists attack X, tested Y way, under Z conditions") are scoped narrowly enough that a specific test COULD disprove them, which is exactly what makes a positive result (the claim survived testing) actually meaningful evidence, rather than an unfalsifiable claim's meaningless "still standing."

3. Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure, Formally

Disclosure modelStructureTradeoff
Full disclosurePublish immediately, no advance vendor noticeForces urgency but leaves users unprotected the moment details are public, before any fix exists
Coordinated (responsible) disclosureNotify vendor privately first; publish publicly only after a fix ships or a reasonable window elapsesProtects users during the fix window, but depends on the vendor actually acting — hence the standard 90-day norm as a backstop against indefinite vendor silence
Non-disclosureNever publish at allAvoids ever tipping off attackers, but also means other researchers/defenders never benefit from the finding, and the vendor may have no external pressure to fix it

The 90-day convention exists specifically to resolve the tension in coordinated disclosure: it gives a vendor real time to develop and ship a fix, while preventing a vendor from simply never responding and indefinitely leaving users unprotected with the researcher unable to ever warn anyone. disclosure_timeline_checker.py's framing — a norm to justify deviating from, not an absolute rule — reflects that this window is a widely-adopted convention, not a universal legal requirement.

4. The CTF Workflow as a Formal Loop

Every CTF challenge, regardless of category, follows the same abstract loop: RECOGNIZE (what category/technique does this problem resemble?), APPLY (execute the relevant known technique), RECOVER (get a candidate answer), VERIFY (confirm it's actually correct, not just plausible), SUBMIT. This module's crackme.c exercises this loop end to end using a technique you already had (Module 20's XOR brute-force) specifically so the loop ITSELF — not the technique — is what's being practiced. Real CTF competitiveness comes from having many techniques in step 2's toolkit (per ctf_progression_guide.md's category breakdown) and getting FAST at steps 1 and 4 — recognizing a challenge's category quickly, and verifying an answer's correctness before wasting time on a wrong path.

Comprehensive exam framing: "What is the relationship between a CTF flag-recovery workflow and a formal research methodology?" Both are instances of the same underlying epistemic structure: form a specific, checkable claim (a flag; a hypothesis), gather evidence through a defined, reproducible process (brute-force search + binary verification; an experiment), and only accept the claim once independently verified (the challenge's own exit code; peer review/reproducibility). The surface domains differ completely, but the justification structure — claim, method, independent verification — is identical, which is precisely why CTF practice is genuine (if narrow) preparation for the broader epistemic discipline research and a comprehensive exam both require.
